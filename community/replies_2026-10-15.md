## u/iSnake37 (2026-10-12 20:43 UTC, reply to post)

ok mate couple of things 

first, trend has this property where it's returns scale almost infinitely with the square root of independent bets, or to put it simply the more shit you trade it on the more up-and-to-the-right your equity curve will be (higher Sharpe ratio)

trend following on only 2 coins BTC & ETH is pointless, gotta do it on a whole basket of assets at the same time (quants call it a "trading universe"). that's because you never actually know for certain what assets will trend, so you cast a larger net to increase your chances, that's how you make money 

it's also the case that trend works best on high liquidity + low volatility coins. knowing this, you can construct a trading universe with say top ~30 coins by market cap. or even top 50 if you have the margin for it. to make trend work below top 50... you'd need to adjust for things that are fucking with you, this is what all the stat arb nerds talk about regressing to factors on residuals etc etc. anyways i'm not going into that here

since you're just starting out, forget all the unnecessary shit man, you don't need any backtests etc. literally come up with most simple rules imaginable that you feel like would catch trends, say a simple ma crossover. that's it. trade the signal across entire universe, rebalance portfolio every couple of weeks/month to keep size roughly equal across all positions. no take profits, no stop losses. you enter on your signal and get out/exit on opposite. that would be a toddlers sketch of a real system but it would still make some money. just start trading it, put real money on the line and eventually new ideas will come up which you can explore. for a more "proper" way to build trend systems you can check out rob carvers books, you can start with "leveraged trading" and "systematic trading". 

i know you've mentioned 4h, just use daily bars. for trend, forget about anything below daily. ltf you get mean reversion that dominates. even the smartest people i know in the space run theirs crypto trend with 9 figures using daily bars, they just refactor them more frequently. YOU can't do that (yet) because transaction costs will eat you alive. profits are uncertain, while trading costs are. so keep your trading to the minimum needed to monetise your edge.

## u/Huge_Position890 (2026-10-13 09:41 UTC, reply to post)

This post could have been mine. I audited my own pipeline a while back and found I'd been fooling myself too, in a different way. Starting over with a real holdout is the hard part, and you've already done it.

**1. When to trust / pull the plug.** At a 25% win rate, 4 losses in your first 4 trades happens about 32% of the time. I simulated a year of your setup (\~60 trades): the longest losing streak is typically **10**, one year in ten has **16**, and 1% of years have **24**. Even if your true win rate is exactly 25%, luck alone gives you anywhere from 10 to 21 winners in that year.

So P&L won't tell you much for a long time. What helped me: write the kill rule down *before* paper trading starts, based on your own backtest (e.g. "stop if the losing streak or drawdown goes past the 99th percentile of what the backtest produces"), and don't change it afterwards. Meanwhile track things that pile up faster than trades: do live entries match what the backtest would have done, and how far are fills from the modelled price?

Also, judge it by average gain per trade (in R), not win rate. I had a filter that looked useless by hit rate and was doing its work in the size of the losses.

**3. BTC + ETH.** They move together, especially in sell-offs, so two markets isn't two independent bets. More crypto markets mostly gives you more trades of the same bet. And check your backtest in separate periods (I use five slices). Trend systems can look great over the whole history and flip sign between periods.

**2.** Can't help on US venues, I'm not US-based. If you go with futures, look at how funding behaved over time, not just the fee table.

Good luck with the fresh start.
